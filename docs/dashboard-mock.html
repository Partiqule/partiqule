v8: everyday user pass

Same intent ("what's in my home, and what do I do about it?"), different vocabulary. Plain language, impact-led, action available in two taps.

  1. "Items in your home" replaces "Your shelf." Implies ownership, not history of scans. Backed by a new ownership state on the data layer (default new scans to owned, with a "just looking" toggle at scan time).
  2. "Edit what you own" link added inline. One tap to clean up scanned-but-not-owned items. Lightweight UX promise that the count actually means something.
  3. Findings rewritten in plain language with impact framing. Out: "PFAS," "NOVA 4," "phthalates," "banned in the EU." In: "linked to hormone disruption," "highly processed foods," "linked to fertility risks," "banned in other countries." Each row shows the count of cleaner swaps available, so the user goes from problem awareness to action path in one line. Tap a row to see a filtered list of those products with cleaner alternatives ready to swap, with bulk-swap available when alternatives exist for all.
  4. "Today's swap" replaces "Worst on your shelf." Skip / Grab pattern direct from the voice kit. Specific product, specific replacement, specific scores. The footer shows "1 of 14 to swap," nudging to the broader queue.
  5. Legend label "to fix" became "to swap." Action verb, parallel to other CTAs.
Removed: the big "What are you looking at?" scan card. Scan FAB at the bottom carries the affordance now. Home becomes a true dashboard.
