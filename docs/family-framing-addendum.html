Strategic Addendum · May 2026

Partiqule is for families.

A positioning shift from individual-consumer product safety to whole-household, life-stage-aware family intelligence. This document captures what changes, what stays the same, and how every surface should reflect the shift.

The shift in one sentence

Partiqule is for the parent who actually reads labels — and the rest of us who do too. The user might be one adult, but the purpose is the household: kids, partners, grandparents, anyone whose product exposure matters to the person doing the scanning.

User identity: kid-centric, life-stage-aware. Parents are the most activated buyers (highest willingness-to-pay for family safety) and life stages create natural product arcs (pregnancy → newborn → toddler → school-age → teen). Non-parent adults are not excluded; the framing makes room for them, but parents lead.

What changes

Voice and tone

The voice kit's "knowing friend reading labels" framing stays intact. What shifts is the implicit reader. The tone is now adult-to-adult about products that affect people the reader cares about, not adult-to-adult about products that affect the reader.

Old framingNew framing
"What's in the products you use." "What's in the products your family uses."
"Scan it. Score it. Skip it." No change — three-beat tagline still works.
"Banned in Hawaii. Sold here." No change — receipts framing still works.
"Save your items and never lose them." "Save what's in your home so you remember next time."
"Worth knowing." No change — eyebrow stays.

Voice rules to add

  • "Your family" lands more than "you" when describing risk. "3 items are linked to hormone disruption" → "3 items in your home are linked to hormone disruption" → "3 items affecting your family are linked to hormone disruption." The third reads as the most activated.
  • Kid-specific scoring should surface visibly when relevant. If an item scored 5 for adults and 7 for kids, the gap matters and should be visible. Don't hide the kid-specific number behind a toggle.
  • Avoid "mom" or "parent" as the gendered identity hook. The voice kit already forbids "mama" and "mom hack." Family framing makes this rule more important, not less. The reader is whoever does the shopping in the household.
  • Life-stage references are okay when specific. "If your kid is in school" or "before your daughter was born" or "during pregnancy" are concrete and actionable. "For your family" without specificity is generic.

Voice rules to keep enforcing

  • No first person ("I", "we") — applies as before.
  • No moralizing or panic framing — applies more strongly now. Family framing makes panic content more tempting and more harmful.
  • Receipts over claims — applies as before. "3 items in your home are linked to hormone disruption" is a receipt. "Protect your family from toxins" is a claim and forbidden.
  • Villainize labels and regulatory gaps, not shoppers or parents — applies as before. "The label doesn't have to tell you" is correct. "You should have known better" is wrong, and even more wrong when speaking to a parent who already worries.

Findings calibration map — additions

The current spec lists four finding categories: banned in other countries, highly processed foods, hormone disruption, fertility risks. Family framing adds a fifth and reframes one.

  • New: Kid-elevated risk. "N items in your home score higher for kids than adults." Already supported by current calibration via kid_score_floor on most entries. Can ship today without new calibration buckets. This becomes the headline family-aware finding.
  • Reframed: Fertility risks. Currently positioned as personal-fertility concern. Family framing reframes as "items linked to fertility and developmental risks" — broadens to include developmental concerns affecting kids in addition to reproductive concerns affecting adults trying to conceive.
  • Other categories unchanged. Banned in other countries, highly processed foods, hormone disruption all work under family framing without modification.

Sequencing implication

Kid-elevated risk finding can ship today. Unlike hormone disruption or fertility risks (which require calibration buckets that don't exist yet), kid-elevated risk is computable from current calibration via kid_score_floor data. This becomes a strong candidate for Track 4 launch even under the narrower Path B sequencing — the family-framing finding lands first because it doesn't depend on new calibration buckets.

Content strategy with Sara

Family framing opens content angles that individual-consumer framing didn't activate. These are higher-engagement because they tap into family-protective instincts.

Content angles to develop

  • "Swaps for the diaper bag" — recurring series like Sunday Swaps but specifically for items new parents pack. High share rate among new-parent audiences.
  • "What's in your kid's lunchbox vs theirs" — comparison content showing kid-marketed products against the same category for adults. Often surprising.
  • "Back-to-school cleanout" — seasonal series about reviewing kid products at school year start.
  • "Products you bought before your daughter was born that are now banned in EU" — life-stage-aware framing of regulatory gaps. Reads as receipts, not panic.
  • "Toddler safe doesn't mean toddler clean" — distinguishing safety certifications (no choking hazards) from chemical safety (ingredient-level concerns).
  • "What changed when you became a parent that should change your shopping list" — cumulative-exposure framing for new parents.

Content rules to enforce

  • No fear-mongering imagery of children. Kids in content should look healthy and present, not vulnerable or in distress. The voice kit's "no panic framing" rule applies even more strongly when kids are visible.
  • Specific products beat generic categories. "Swap Banana Boat Sport for Blue Lizard Mineral" beats "swap your sunscreen for a mineral one." Receipts framing applies.
  • Don't moralize about parenting choices. A parent who buys conventional baby wash is a parent who hasn't seen the data, not a parent who failed. Tone is informational, not judgmental.
  • Pregnancy content carries extra responsibility. Pregnancy is a high-anxiety life stage. Content should be specific, evidence-grounded, and free of speculative claims. When in doubt, defer to OB/midwife guidance.

Growth strategy

Family framing changes audience targeting and partnership avenues.

Audience targeting shifts

  • Primary: parents of kids 0–12, with sub-segments for pregnancy, infants/toddlers, school-age. Higher willingness-to-pay than individual-consumer wellness audiences.
  • Secondary: parents of teens and young adults still in the household. Different product categories (personal care, food, dorm-bound items) but same activated-buyer pattern.
  • Tertiary: safety-conscious adults without kids. Not excluded, not led with. Content and copy should not make this audience feel ignored.

Partnership avenues

  • Pediatric content creators (RNs, nurse practitioners, MDs who do kid-focused content)
  • Family-focused brands (clean kid product lines, organic baby food, mineral sunscreen brands marketed to families)
  • Parenting publications (Romper, The Bump, Motherly, Cup of Jo for the older-millennial-parent demographic)
  • Kid-product retailers (Babylist registry partnerships, Maisonette curation partnerships)
  • Pregnancy-specific channels (OB practices, doula networks, prenatal yoga studios — small but high-trust)

What does NOT change

Family framing is a positioning and content shift. The technical foundation is unchanged.

  • Calibration evidence work. Sucralose's evidence base is sucralose's evidence base regardless of who reads the score. Citation discipline, regulatory verification, score positioning relative to other entries — all unchanged.
  • Engine architecture. The deterministic scoring engine produces scores per substance. Family framing affects how findings are presented in the dashboard, not how scores are computed. Phase 2 promotion timeline unchanged.
  • Technical implementation patterns. Blast-radius rules, surgical patching, pre-push verification, schema consistency discipline — all unchanged.
  • App architecture. Capacitor bundled mode, Railway/Vercel deploy patterns, Supabase data layer — all unchanged.
  • The 1–10 scoring system itself. Lower is better. Color thresholds (green ≤3, amber ≤5, red >5). All unchanged. Family framing just makes the kid_score_floor work more visible than it was.

The shift is real but the work is incremental. Nothing about today's calibration buildout, engine architecture, or technical execution changes. What changes is the implicit reader and the framing of what the product is for. That framing gets baked into copy, content, and design as new surfaces are built — not retrofitted into surfaces that already work.
